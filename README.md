## 1. System Architecture

RiskLattice operates on a **strict neuro-symbolic division of labor** designed for institutional financial risk environments.

---

### 1.1 What is "Neuro-Symbolic Division"?

In computer science and artificial intelligence:
- **"Neuro" (Connectionist / Deep Learning / LLMs)** refers to probabilistic artificial neural networks. They excel at **perception, semantic understanding, and unstructured text parsing** (e.g., reading a messy 100-page SEC 10-K filing, or understanding a colloquial prompt like *"what happens if a typhoon shuts down Taiwanese ports?"*). However, neural networks are fundamentally probabilistic token predictors: they **hallucinate**, cannot reliably perform matrix multiplication, lack exact floating-point precision, and fail regulatory auditability.
- **"Symbolic" (Classical Logic / Deterministic Mathematics / Graph Theory)** refers to systems based on **explicit mathematical equations, graph algorithms, and formal logic**. They excel at **rigorous, bit-exact, deterministic computation** (e.g., sparse matrix message passing, Huber loss regressions, Copula probability integrals, and portfolio balance-sheet revaluations). However, symbolic systems are completely brittle when facing unstructured real-world language: they cannot read an annual report or interpret breaking geopolitical news on their own.

**"Neuro-Symbolic Division of Labor"** is the architectural principle of strictly assigning each task exclusively to the paradigm that is mathematically and practically suited for it:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                NEURO-SYMBOLIC DIVISION OF LABOR IN RISKLATTICE                         │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│               "NEURO" (Semantic AI)             │             "SYMBOLIC" (Deterministic Math)          │
│          Probabilistic • Semantic • Fuzzy       │            Deterministic • Verifiable • Bit-Exact    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Ingests unstructured SEC 10-K disclosures     │ • 4-hop sparse matrix graph message-passing (W)      │
│ • Translates natural language scenarios to s^(0)│ • Outlier-resistant Huber regression calibration     │
│ • Synthesizes executive narrative summaries     │ • Non-linear corporate leverage multipliers (lambda) │
│ • Generates contextual tail-risk hedge ideas    │ • 10,000-path Student-t Copula Monte Carlo (nu = 5)  │
│ • Must pass verbatim source substring checks    │ • Non-linear portfolio PnL & waterfall attribution   │
│                                                 │ • Basel / Kupiec POF regulatory statistical tests    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ❌ THE NEURAL TIER NEVER DOES MATH: Cannot multiply, cannot simulate, cannot touch portfolio dollars.  │
│ ❌ THE SYMBOLIC TIER NEVER PARSES TEXT: Operates purely on typed numbers, matrices, and DataFrames.   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Why This Division is Mandatory in Institutional Finance:
1. **Regulatory Auditability (Basel III, Dodd-Frank, SEC)**: Risk managers must explain every basis point of capital reserve to bank examiners. An LLM generating an unverified loss figure is illegal under model risk management standards (SR 11-7). In RiskLattice, every Value at Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (CVaR) number is generated by deterministic NumPy/SciPy math that can be audited down to the individual floating-point bit.
2. **Zero-Hallucination Guarantees**: By physically isolating the mathematical engine from the LLM via Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) unit tests, the LLM cannot hallucinate portfolio losses, change graph weights, or alter risk distributions.
3. **Local Privacy Firewall (Rule P-01)**: Hedge funds and asset managers cannot upload client position sizes, cash balances, or trade blotters to external AI models. Because the neural tier only handles semantic translation, private holdings never leave the symbolic DuckDB database.

## 2. What All Is Being Stress-Tested in RiskLattice?

Traditional enterprise risk management systems (such as standard Parametric Value at Risk, 60-day historical simulation, or linear multi-factor models like Barra and Axioma) suffer from a critical foundational flaw: **they assume the financial world is linear, normally distributed, continuous, and static**. They ask: *"If the benchmark drops 2%, what is our portfolio drawdown based on trailing linear covariances?"*

In real-world financial crises (the 2008 Lehman interbank freeze, the 2020 COVID mobility halt, the 2021 Suez Canal blockage, the 2022 duration shock, or a sudden Taiwan Strait naval quarantine), **linear correlation models fail catastrophically**. Correlations jump toward 1.0, multi-tier supply chains fracture, market liquidity evaporates in fire sales, and leveraged balance sheets hit sudden, non-linear solvency cliffs.

### 2.1 Layer 1: Multi-Hop Network Contagion (Hops 1 to 4)
- **The Blind Spot in Traditional Models**: Standard models (Barra, RiskMetrics) only evaluate direct, 1st-order counterparty links or aggregate sector betas. If an asset manager does not directly hold TSMC shares, their portfolio's historical beta to Taiwanese semiconductors appears benign ($\beta \approx 0.12$).
- **What RiskLattice Tests**: Recursive multi-tier contagion through directed dependency graphs using vectorized SciPy CSR sparse matrix message-passing:
  $$\mathbf{in}^{(k)} = \Delta \mathbf{s}^{(k-1)} \cdot W^T, \quad \Delta s_v^{(k)} = \alpha^{k-1} \cdot \text{in}_v^{(k)} \cdot (1 - \sigma_v) \cdot \lambda_v$$
  Transmission progresses systematically across 4 hops:
  $$\text{Hop 0 (Direct Shock)} \longrightarrow \text{Hop 1 (Tier-1 Suppliers)} \longrightarrow \text{Hop 2 (Downstream OEMs)} \longrightarrow \text{Hop 3/4 (Network Contagion)}$$
- **Real-World Case**: Automotive manufacturers (Ford, General Motors) do not purchase silicon wafers directly from TSMC; they purchase microcontroller modules from Tier-1 suppliers (NXP Semiconductors, Infineon, Bosch). When TSMC halts, the shock ripples through Tier-1 suppliers to Tier-2 assembly plants, halting automotive assembly lines worldwide. RiskLattice uncovers these hidden multi-hop transmission channels.

### 2.2 Layer 2: Multi-Asset Non-Linear Portfolio Valuation & Greeks
- **The Blind Spot in Traditional Models**: Traditional linear models revalue portfolios using simple first-order delta approximations ($\Delta P_i \approx \text{Notional}_i \cdot \Delta S_i$). This severely misprices instruments with embedded convexity, asymmetric payoffs, or credit spreads.
- **What RiskLattice Tests**: Full non-linear asset-class revaluation across **7 distinct institutional instrument types**:
  1. **Equities (Long/Short)**: Convex beta-gamma response $\Delta P_i = N_i \cdot (\beta_i S_i + \frac{1}{2} \gamma_i S_i^2)$.
  2. **Stock Options (Long/Short Calls & Puts)**: Black-Scholes Greeks revaluation including Delta ($\Delta$), Gamma ($\Gamma$), Vega ($\nu$), and Theta ($\Theta$):
     $$\Delta P_{\text{opt}} \approx \Delta \cdot \Delta S + \frac{1}{2} \Gamma \cdot (\Delta S)^2 + \nu \cdot \Delta \sigma_{\text{implied}}$$
  3. **Corporate Bonds**: Duration-convexity pricing coupled with Merton structural credit spread widening:
     $$\Delta P_{\text{bond}} \approx -D_{\text{mod}} \cdot \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} C \cdot (\Delta y)^2, \quad \Delta y = \Delta y_{\text{macro}} + \alpha_{\text{credit}} \cdot |S_v|$$
  4. **Credit Default Swaps (CDS)**: Mark-to-market hazard rate revaluation:
     $$\Delta P_{\text{CDS}} \approx N \cdot \text{RPV01} \cdot (\Delta s_{\text{CDS}}), \quad \Delta s_{\text{CDS}} = (1 - R) \cdot \Delta \lambda_{\text{hazard}}$$
  5. **Private Credit**: Illiquidity haircuts and covenant violation penalties ($15\%$ performing haircut, $35\%$ distressed haircut when leverage breaches covenant thresholds).
  6. **FX Forwards**: Cross-currency basis and covered interest rate parity shifts.
  7. **Collateralized Loan Obligations (CLO Tranches)**: Subordination waterfall cash-flow impairment (Equity tranche absorbed first, then Mezzanine, then Senior AAA).
- **Real-World Case**: A portfolio holding long equity in Nvidia, short put options on AMD, and long corporate bonds in Ford faces simultaneous equity losses, accelerating gamma losses on short puts as volatility spikes, and bond mark-to-market losses as Ford's credit spreads widen. Linear models miss over 40% of this drawdown.

### 2.6 Layer 6: Safety Stock Days-of-Inventory (DOI) Absorption & Depletion Kinetics
- **The Blind Spot in Traditional Models**: Assumes every supply disruption immediately impacts production on Day 1, ignoring on-hand physical inventory cushions.
- **What RiskLattice Tests**: Dynamic physical buffer absorption:
  $$S_{\text{eff}} = \max\left(0, S_{\text{gross}} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{\text{Days}_{\text{Inv}}}{\text{Duration}}\right)\right)$$
  - If a company holds 90 days of safety stock and a supplier outage lasts 30 days, $S_{\text{eff}} = 0$ (the buffer absorbs 100% of the shock).
  - If the disruption lasts 120 days, the buffer cushions the first 90 days and transmits the remaining 25% of the shock.
- **Real-World Case**: In 2021, Toyota navigated the initial global automotive semiconductor shortage for 4 months because its post-Fukushima "Business Continuity Plan" mandated 120 days of chip buffer, while competitors with just-in-time (JIT) 14-day inventories halted plants within two weeks.

### 2.7 Layer 7: Endogenous Market Impact & Fire-Sale Liquidity Spirals

### 2.8 Layer 8: Extreme Tail Dependence & Correlation Breakdown (Student-$t$ Copula)
- **The Blind Spot in Traditional Models**: Gaussian (Normal) Value at Risk assumes joint tail crashes are virtually impossible ($6\sigma$ events occurring once in a universe's lifetime). In crises, Gaussian models vastly understate risk.
- **What RiskLattice Tests**: Multivariate Student-$t$ Copula with $\nu = 4.0$ degrees of freedom and Higham spectral positive semi-definite (PSD) eigenvalue regularization:
  - Models non-zero asymptotic lower tail dependence: $\lambda_L = 2 t_{\nu+1}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{(\nu+1)(1-\rho)}{1+\rho}}\right) > 0$.
  - 10,000 Monte Carlo paths simulate realistic joint crash clustering.
  - Computes subadditive 99% Expected Shortfall (CVaR) and position-by-position Marginal Contribution to Risk (MCR).
  - Quantifies the **Tail Ratio** ($\text{CVaR}_{\text{Copula}} / \text{VaR}_{\text{Gaussian}} \approx 12.2\times$), proving tail under-insurance.
- **Real-World Case**: In October 2008 and March 2020, diversified portfolios of tech, banks, energy, and municipal bonds crashed together as correlations jumped from 0.25 to 0.85. RiskLattice captures this joint lower-tail clustering.
- **Documentation Guide**: Detailed in [**Student-t Copula Tail-Risk Engine**](docs/features/02_student_t_copula_tail_risk.md) and [**ADR-005: Copula Simulation**](docs/adrs/ADR-005-risk.md).

### 2.9 Layer 9: Algorithmic Reverse Stress Testing & Minimal Breaking Points
- **The Blind Spot in Traditional Models**: Traditional risk models ask: *"Given this scenario, what is our loss?"* They cannot answer the existential executive question: *"What is the smallest plausible combination of shocks that would render our firm insolvent?"*
- **What RiskLattice Tests**: Formulates an algorithmic inverse optimization problem:
  $$\min_{\mathbf{s}} \|\mathbf{s}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \text{Drawdown}(\mathbf{s}) \ge D_{\text{target}}, \quad \mathbf{s}_{\text{min}} \le \mathbf{s} \le \mathbf{s}_{\text{max}}$$
  Finds the minimal-norm systemic shock vector $\mathbf{s}^*$ that forces the portfolio past a catastrophic drawdown threshold (e.g. $-30\%$), exposing hidden structural vulnerabilities that forward scenarios miss.
- **Real-World Case**: Uncovers that an asset manager's portfolio can survive a 50% drop in crude oil alone, but a concurrent $-18\%$ wafer disruption combined with a $+40\text{ bps}$ SOFR spike triggers simultaneous cross-asset margin calls and covenant haircuts, breaching capital limits.
- **Documentation Guide**: Detailed in [**Reverse Stress Testing**](docs/features/07_reverse_stress_testing.md).

### 2.10 Layer 10: What-If Supply Chain Graph Interventions & Resilience ROI

## 7. The Core Algorithms: Contagion Propagation & Tail-Risk Engines

At the computational heart of RiskLattice are **two deterministic algorithms** executed in sequence by the pure Python math engine:

```

│                              THE TWO CORE ALGORITHMS IN RISKLATTICE                                    │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ALGORITHM 1: 4-HOP DAMPED CONTAGION ENGINE      │ ALGORITHM 2: STUDENT-t COPULA TAIL-RISK ENGINE       │
│ "The Network Domino Simulator"                  │ "The Joint Crash & Extreme Tail-Risk Simulator"      │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Vectorized sparse-matrix propagation         │ 1. Regularized spectral eigenvalue PSD repair        │
│ 2. Exponential hop damping (alpha = 0.65)       │ 2. Cholesky factorization of correlation matrix      │
│ 3. Supplier substitutability resistance (1 - s) │ 3. Correlated multivariate standard normal sampling  │

```

### 7.1 Algorithm 1: Damped Network Contagion Propagation

**Purpose**: Computes how an initial macro impulse $s^{(0)}$ cascades through supply chains, customer linkages, and credit channels across up to 4 network hops.

#### Mathematical Formulation & Step-by-Step Execution

Given directed graph $G = (V, E, W)$ with $N = 50$ nodes and adjacency sensitivity matrix $W \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ calibrated via Huber robust loss ($L_\delta$):

1. **Input Clamping**: The initial shock vector $s^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is clamped to invariant bounds:
   $$s_v^{(0)} = \text{clip}\left(s_v^{(0)}, -0.95, +2.00\right), \quad \forall v \in V$$
2. **Pre-computing Balance-Sheet Distress Multipliers ($\lambda_v$)**:
   Each node's balance sheet is evaluated against corporate leverage triggers:
   $$\lambda_v = \begin{cases} 1 + 0.35 \cdot \max(0, \text{NetDebt/EBITDA}_v - 3.0), & \text{if } v \in \{\text{ASSET}, \text{SUPPLIER}\} \text{ and } (\text{Debt/EBITDA} > 3.0 \text{ or } \text{ICR} < 1.5) \\ 1.0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
3. **Iterative Matrix Message Passing ($k = 1, 2, 3, 4$)**:
   At each hop $k$, the incoming impulse from predecessor nodes is computed via vector-matrix dot product:
   $$\mathbf{in}^{(k)} = \Delta \mathbf{s}^{(k-1)} \cdot W \quad \implies \quad \text{in}_v^{(k)} = \sum_{u=1}^N \Delta s_u^{(k-1)} W_{uv}$$
4. **Damping, Substitutability, and Asymmetric Amplification**:
   For each node $v$, the transmitted shock is damped by hop factor $\alpha^{k-1}$ ($\alpha = 0.65$), scaled by vendor non-substitutability $(1 - \sigma_v)$, and amplified by leverage multiplier $\lambda_v$ only if the shock is negative:
   $$\Delta s_v^{(k)} = \begin{cases} 0.0, & \text{if } |\text{in}_v^{(k)}| < 10^{-4} \text{ (Convergence threshold)} \\ \alpha^{k-1} \cdot \text{in}_v^{(k)} \cdot (1 - \sigma_v) \cdot \lambda_v, & \text{if } \text{in}_v^{(k)} < 0 \\ \alpha^{k-1} \cdot \text{in}_v^{(k)} \cdot (1 - \sigma_v) \cdot 1.0, & \text{if } \text{in}_v^{(k)} \ge 0 \end{cases}$$
5. **Cumulative Clamping & Order Attribution**:
   Total accumulated shock $S_v$ is bounded to prevent infinite economic destruction:
   $$S_v = \text{clip}\left(\sum_{k=0}^4 \Delta s_v^{(k)}, -0.95, +1.50\right)$$
   The shock is cleanly decomposed into an additive attribution ledger:
   $$\text{Order}_1 = \Delta s_v^{(0)} + \Delta s_v^{(1)} \quad (\text{Direct}), \qquad \text{Order}_2 = \Delta s_v^{(2)} \quad (\text{Tier-1 Suppliers}), \qquad \text{Order}_{3/4} = \Delta s_v^{(3)} + \Delta s_v^{(4)} \quad (\text{Network Contagion})$$

### 7.2 Algorithm 2: Conditioned Student-$t$ Copula Monte Carlo Tail Risk

**Purpose**: Simulates 10,000 joint portfolio return paths under heavy-tailed market conditions conditioned on the post-contagion shock vector $S_v$, computing regulatory-grade Value at Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (CVaR).

#### Mathematical Formulation & Step-by-Step Execution

1. **Correlation Matrix Regularization & Spectral PSD Repair**:
   Given empirical asset return matrix $R \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times K}$, compute sample correlation matrix $\mathbf{R}_{\text{corr}}$. To prevent non-positive definite breakdown during crises:
   $$\mathbf{R}_{\text{reg}} = \mathbf{R}_{\text{corr}} + 10^{-6}\mathbf{I}_K$$
   Perform eigenvalue decomposition $\mathbf{R}_{\text{reg}} = \mathbf{V} \mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{V}^T$, clamp all eigenvalues to positive floor $\lambda_i = \max(\lambda_i, 10^{-6})$, and renormalize diagonals to 1.0:
   $$\mathbf{R}_{\text{PSD}} = \mathbf{V} \text{diag}(\max(\mathbf{\lambda}, 10^{-6})) \mathbf{V}^T, \quad \mathbf{R}_{\text{PSD}} = \mathbf{D}^{-1/2} \mathbf{R}_{\text{PSD}} \mathbf{D}^{-1/2}$$
2. **Cholesky Factorization**:
   Compute lower-triangular matrix $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ such that $\mathbf{L} \mathbf{L}^T = \mathbf{R}_{\text{PSD}}$.
3. **Multivariate Student-$t$ Generation**:
   - Sample uncorrelated standard normal matrix $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times K}$ ($M = 10,000$ paths) using pinned `PCG64(seed=42)`.
   - Impose cross-asset correlations: $\mathbf{Z}_{\text{corr}} = \mathbf{Z} \mathbf{L}^T$.
   - Sample radial scaling factor from Chi-square distribution with $\nu = 4.0$ (or $5.0$) degrees of freedom:
     $$W_m \sim \chi^2(\nu), \quad \text{scale}_m = \sqrt{\frac{\nu}{W_m}}, \quad \forall m \in \{1, \dots, M\}$$
   - Form heavy-tailed multivariate Student-$t$ variates:
     $$\mathbf{T}_{\text{samples}} = \mathbf{Z}_{\text{corr}} \odot \mathbf{scale}$$
4. **Contagion Conditioning**:
   Scale standard Student-$t$ variates by asset empirical volatilities $\mathbf{\sigma}_{\text{assets}}$ and add the deterministic contagion shock vector $\mathbf{S}_{\text{contagion}}$ from Algorithm 1:
   $$\mathbf{R}_{\text{simulated}} = \left(\mathbf{T}_{\text{samples}} \odot \mathbf{\sigma}_{\text{assets}}\right) + \mathbf{S}_{\text{contagion}}$$
5. **Portfolio Loss Distribution & Coherent Risk Metrics**:
   Given normalized portfolio weights $\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^K$, compute simulated portfolio losses $L_m = -(\mathbf{R}_{\text{simulated}, m} \cdot \mathbf{w})$:
   - **Value at Risk (99% VaR)**: The 99th empirical percentile of losses:
     $$\text{VaR}_{0.99} = \text{Quantile}_{0.99}(\{L_1, \dots, L_M\})$$
   - **Expected Shortfall (99% CVaR)**: The conditional average loss in the tail:
     $$\text{CVaR}_{0.99} = \frac{1}{| \{m : L_m \ge \text{VaR}_{0.99}\} |} \sum_{L_m \ge \text{VaR}_{0.99}} L_m$$
   - **Marginal Contribution to Risk (MCR)**: Evaluates asset-level risk sensitivity:
     $$\text{MCR}_i = \frac{\partial \text{CVaR}_{0.99}}{\partial w_i} \approx \mathbb{E}\left[ -R_{m, i} \mid L_m \ge \text{VaR}_{0.99} \right]$$
