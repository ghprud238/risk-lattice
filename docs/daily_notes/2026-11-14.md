### 2.1 Tail-Risk Hedge Optimization (Convex SLSQP)
The institutional hedge allocation problem is formulated as constrained optimization:
$$\max_{x \in \mathbb{R}^M} \quad \sum_{j=1}^M x_j \cdot \Delta \Pi_j(S) - \sum_{j=1}^M x_j \cdot c_j$$
subject to:
$$\sum_{j=1}^M x_j \cdot c_j \le B_{\text{budget}}$$
$$0 \le x_j \le \text{Notional}_{\max, j} \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, M\}$$
where:
- $x_j$ is the allocated notional to hedge candidate $j$.
- $c_j$ is the annualized option premium or CDS basis spread.
- $\Delta \Pi_j(S) = |S_{\text{target}}| \cdot \eta_j$ is the asymmetric payout elasticity of the derivative contract under network shock $S$.
- $B_{\text{budget}}$ is the Chief Investment Officer's dollar hedging budget limit.

### 2.2 Reverse Stress Testing Formulation
Standard stress testing evaluates $S \mapsto \Delta \text{PnL}$. Reverse stress testing inverts this mapping to find the smallest perturbation in macro factors that triggers a designated capital threshold $\theta_{\text{drawdown}}$:
$$\min_{f \in \mathbb{R}^K} \quad \|f\|_2^2 = \sum_{k=1}^K f_k^2$$
subject to:
$$\sum_{k=1}^K \beta_k \cdot f_k \ge \theta_{\text{drawdown}}$$
$$0 \le f_k \le f_{\max, k} \quad \forall k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$$
The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) optimality conditions yield the minimal shock vector:
$$f_k^* = \lambda^* \cdot \frac{\beta_k}{2}$$
where $\lambda^*$ is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the portfolio loss constraint. The critical factor is determined by maximal marginal loss contribution:
$$k^* = \arg\max_k \left( \beta_k \cdot f_k^* \right)$$

### 2.3 Chronological Propagation Velocity & Inventory Dynamics
In multi-period simulation ($t \in \{0, 7, 14, 30, 45, 60, 90\}$ days):
1. **Transit Delay**: The contagion wavefront reaches node $v$ located at topological shortest distance $d(v)$ from the shock root at:
   $$t_{\text{arrival}}(v) = d(v) \cdot 7 \text{ days}$$
2. **Buffer Absorption**: For $t \ge t_{\text{arrival}}(v)$, the remaining safety inventory buffer is:
   $$\tau_{\text{rem}}(v, t) = \max\left(0.0, \, \tau_{\text{inv}}(v) - (t - t_{\text{arrival}}(v))\right)$$
3. **Solvency Escalation Multiplier**: Once inventory is exhausted ($\tau_{\text{rem}} = 0$), fixed manufacturing overhead, idle labor costs, and lost sales compound exponentially:
   $$\mu_v(t) = 1.0 + 0.005 \cdot \left(t - t_{\text{arrival}}(v) - \tau_{\text{inv}}(v)\right)$$
   $$S_v(t) = \min\left(-0.95, \, S_v^{\text{raw}} \cdot \mu_v(t)\right)$$
