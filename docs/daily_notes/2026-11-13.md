└──────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┘

### 2.1 Equity Options (Black-Scholes with Volatility Skew)
- **Pricing Formulation**:
  $$d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + (r + 0.5 \sigma^2) T}{\sigma \sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{T}$$
  $$\text{Call} = S \Phi(d_1) - K e^{-r T} \Phi(d_2)$$
  $$\text{Put} = K e^{-r T} \Phi(-d_2) - S \Phi(-d_1)$$
- **Crash Skew Dynamics**:
  $$S_1 = S_0 \cdot (1 + S_v)$$
  $$\sigma_1 = \sigma_0 + \text{vol\_skew} \cdot \max(0, -S_v) \cdot \lambda_v$$
- **Protective Tail-Risk Hedging**: When an underlying crashes ($S_v = -0.25$), a Long Out-of-the-Money Put option surges in value, producing asymmetric positive returns ($> 100\%$) that protect the portfolio.

### 2.2 Corporate Bonds (Duration-Convexity & Credit Spread Widening)
- **Spread Widening**:
  $$\Delta s_{\text{bps}} = \beta_{\text{credit}} \cdot \max(0, -S_v) \cdot \lambda_v \cdot 1000.0, \quad \Delta y = \frac{\Delta s_{\text{bps}}}{10000.0}$$
- **Price Sensitivity**:
  $$\frac{\Delta P}{P} = -D_{\text{mod}} \cdot \Delta y + \frac{1}{2} C \cdot (\Delta y)^2$$
- **Distress Amplification**: High balance-sheet leverage ($\lambda_v > 1.0$) exacerbates credit spread widening and bond price drawdown.

### 2.3 Single-Name Credit Default Swaps (CDS)
- **Implied Hazard Rate**:
  $$\gamma_0 = \frac{s_0 / 10000.0}{1 - R}$$
- **Stressed Hazard Jump**:
  $$\gamma_1 = \gamma_0 \cdot \exp\left(\kappa \cdot \max(0, -S_v) \cdot \lambda_v\right)$$
  $$s_1 = \gamma_1 \cdot (1 - R)$$
- **Mark-to-Market PnL**:
  $$\Delta \text{MtM} = \text{side\_sign} \cdot \text{Notional} \cdot D_{\text{spread}} \cdot (s_1 - s_0)$$
- **BUY Protection**: Position gains positive MtM as the underlying entity's default probability surges.

### 2.4 Private Credit / Direct Lending (Covenant Breaches)
- **Stressed Leverage Ratio**:
  $$L_1 = \frac{L_0}{1.0 - \min(0.85, \max(0, -S_v) \cdot \lambda_v)}$$
- **Covenant Condition**:
  - If $L_1 \le L_{\text{covenant}}$: Compliant; minor credit spread widening penalty.
  - If $L_1 > L_{\text{covenant}}$: **Covenant Breach** triggered.
  - Restructuring Impairment Haircut:
    $$\text{Haircut} = \min\left(1.0 - R, \, 0.08 + 0.12 \cdot (L_1 - L_{\text{covenant}})\right)$$
    $$\Delta \text{PnL} = -\text{Notional} \cdot \text{Haircut}$$

### 2.5 Structured Credit (CLO Tranche Waterfall)
- **Collateral Pool Loss**:
  $$L_{\text{pool}} = \min(1.0, \, |S_v| \cdot \text{scale})$$
- **Tranche Loss Fraction**:
  $$\text{LossRate} = \frac{\min(D, \max(A, L_{\text{pool}})) - A}{D - A}$$
  - Losses below attachment point $A$ are absorbed by subordinated tranches (0% loss to tranche).
  - Losses between $A$ and $D$ impair the tranche proportionally.
  - Losses exceeding detachment point $D$ result in total write-down (100% loss).

## 3. Inventory Buffer Dynamics in Contagion Propagation

In just-in-time supply chains, inventory buffers act as temporary shock dampers. We updated both the high-level `ContagionPropagator.propagate` method and the pure analytical `propagate_kernel`:

```
Supplier Shock ──► [ Buffer Check ] ──► Downstream Shock
                    If Buffer >= Horizon ──► Shock = 0.0 (Absorbed)
                    If Buffer < Horizon  ──► Shock * (Horizon - Buffer) / Horizon
```

### Empirical Test Cases Verified:
1. **Complete Absorption (`test_complete_inventory_absorption`)**:
   - TSMC takes a -30% capacity disruption over a 30-day horizon.
   - Apple holds 45 days of safety stock inventory.
   - **Result**: Apple takes **0.0% shock** ($\Delta s_{\text{AAPL}} = 0.0$).
2. **Partial Absorption (`test_partial_inventory_absorption`)**:
   - Ford holds 10 days of inventory buffer against a 20-day disruption.
   - **Result**: Exactly **50% of the shock is absorbed**; Ford receives half the impulse compared to zero inventory.

## 5. Google Workspace SSO, Institutional Login Screen & RBAC Architecture

### 5.1 Motivation & Enterprise Requirement
- **User Request**: *"do we have a login screen for this that is tied to google login. If not, let's add it"*
- Prior to this milestone, RiskLattice lacked an authentication gate, granting unauthenticated direct access to the simulation canvas.
- To meet institutional B2B enterprise security standards (SOC 2 Type II, Fed SR 11-7 model governance, and client data isolation), a production authentication gateway is required.

### 5.2 Architecture & Implementation Details

1. **Backend Authentication Service (`backend/app/services/auth_service.py`)**:
   - Decodes and validates Google OAuth ID tokens without heavy third-party dependencies.
   - Enforces Role-Based Access Control (RBAC):
     - `CHIEF_RISK_OFFICER`: Full permissions (`["READ", "SIMULATE", "APPROVE", "EXPORT", "MRM_ADMIN", "SETTINGS"]`).
     - `PORTFOLIO_MANAGER`: Desk permissions (`["READ", "SIMULATE", "INGEST_PORTFOLIO", "EXPORT"]`).
     - `ANALYST`: Research permissions (`["READ", "SIMULATE", "DRAFT_SCENARIO"]`).
     - `AUDITOR`: Compliance permissions (`["READ", "AUDIT_LOGS", "VERIFY_PROOFS"]`).
   - Cryptographic session issuance via HMAC-SHA256 tokens (`rl_sess_...`).
2. **API Endpoints (`backend/app/main.py`)**:
   - `POST /api/v1/auth/google`: Authenticates Google JWT credentials or institutional sandbox profiles.
   - `GET /api/v1/auth/me`: Verifies active session token and returns live user profile.
   - `POST /api/v1/auth/logout`: Invalidates session token.
3. **Frontend Login UI (`frontend/src/components/LoginScreen.tsx`)**:
   - Dark-mode institutional portal styled to match RiskLattice aesthetics.
   - Official Google "G" logo SVG button with Google Identity Services (GIS) integration.
   - 1-click Institutional Sandbox Personas (Sarah Chen - CRO @ Citadel, Marcus Vance - PM @ Point72, Elena Rostova - Analyst @ JPMorgan).
   - Displayed security badges: Fed SR 11-7, Rule P-01 Zero Data Egress, AST Isolation.
4. **Context & Header Integration (`frontend/src/context/AuthContext.tsx` & `page.tsx`)**:
   - Manages global auth state, automatically restoring sessions on refresh.
   - Header displays user avatar, name, institutional role badge, and Sign Out action.
